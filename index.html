What this is, and why you can trust it

01

The problem

Rajasthan Royals must spend INR 27.05 crore on six players on 16 December. Scorecards treat a dot ball in the 3rd over the same as one in a tight 19th over, so they hide who actually delivers when a match is decided.

02

How it works

A win-probability model scores every one of deliveries for how much the match hung on it. Each player is then rated only on the balls that mattered, against what an average player does in the same situation.

03

What is different

Most clutch lists stop at a leaderboard. This one rates reliability as well as impact, covers pressure in both innings, shows how sure each rating is, and turns it into a buy plan and a live auction tool for RR.

04

Why trust it

The weights were fixed before testing. The rating was checked on a season it never saw, re-run 22 different ways, re-run with venues removed, and every player carries a 90% range. Where it is weak, the site says so.

Not every ball is equal.

Scorecards add every delivery up the same way. Pressure ratings weigh each one by how much the match hung on it.

The ranking formula

One rating, three parts, every step checkable.

Every player is rated with the same formula. Nothing is hand-adjusted, and the published rating can be rebuilt exactly from the three parts shown on each player's card.

Rating=50+s ×(0.5 × Impact+0.2 × Lift+0.3 × Dependability)
Impact

How much better than average he does on pressure balls, per ball. Each ball is compared with what an average player does in that exact state (runs needed, balls and wickets left), then adjusted for the quality of the bowler or batter he faced.

Lift

Impact on pressure balls minus his impact on ordinary balls. It separates players who raise their game from good players who are simply good all the time.

Dependability

Share of his pressure matches in which he was net positive. It rewards delivering often over one big night: no single match supplies more than 20% of any top-10 batter's positive total.

s

Each part is standardised within the role (mean 0, SD 1), so batters and bowlers land on one scale: 50 is an average rated player, 15 points is one standard deviation. s = .

How well a player's pressure record in half his matches predicts the other half (batters / bowlers). Pressure performance is real but noisy, so every figure is pulled towards average and the order inside each top ten is a guide, not a precise ranking.

Questions

What a selector would ask

And where the model stops.

Is performing under pressure a real skill?
Partly. Split each player's pressure matches into two halves and the halves agree only weakly, so luck plays a big part. That is why every figure is pulled towards average and why match-by-match dependability carries 30% of the rating.
Why are finishers ahead of openers?
They are at the crease when the result is genuinely in the balance: the last overs of a close chase, or an innings that needs rescuing. Openers face more balls early, when one delivery rarely decides a match.
Why does first-innings pressure work differently?
A side batting first does not know what a winning score is, so no single ball moves its win probability far. There, the situation sets the pressure: a side in trouble puts it on the batter, a side on top on the bowler, and a close game in overs 17–20 on both. Those bands are a judgement call.
How are different leagues compared fairly?
Batter and bowler effects are estimated together, so runs against a strong bowler count for more. That puts the IPL, full-member and associate internationals and emerging-team games on one scale. Players also need 150 pressure balls with at least 75 of them in the IPL, 12 pressure matches and a match in 2026.

What is left out?
The model ignores which teams are playing and reduces the pitch to one par score; a separate venue check (above) changes the ratings little. Fielding is not credited. 80 of 883 matches were removed (no ball data, no result, shortened by rain or incomplete), and the pack has no T20 World Cup matches. Role and price are added by judgement.
